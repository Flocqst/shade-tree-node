Did Google block the clean IP at volume? No.

All 1000 requests returned HTTP 200, and the response sizes were uniform: 996 at ~80.3 KB, 4 at ~80.5 KB. A captcha or "unusual traffic" page would be a different, smaller response, so this is the real homepage every time. Over a thousand automated hits from a single egress IP, Google never rate-limited it or served a challenge.
This is the encouraging real-world read: a genuinely clean egress IP serves Google at this volume without tripping bot detection. The single-IP-at-volume limit is real, but the ceiling is higher than a thousand requests.

About 850 of 1000 requests land between 1.4 and 2.0 seconds, median 1.69s. The slow end is a ~1% tail: ten requests over 5 seconds, which is Tor circuit variance (a circuit rebuild or a congested relay), not Google. A direct fetch with no tunnel is ~0.15s.

Drops

Zero. No timeouts, no circuit failures, no gateway errors, no Google blocks. The gateway log corroborated 1000 new PASS, no new DROP.
The only ways a request drops in this system are a bad or missing proof (forged, garbage, and non-member were all rejected in the security run) and a member exceeding its per-epoch budget (4-of-8 throttled at a budget of 4 there). Neither applied here: one enrolled member, valid proof every time, and the budget was raised to 5000 for the run so the rate limiter would not mask transport reliability. Restored to 100 after.

Read honestly. These are warm, steady-state numbers. The first request after a client starts pays a one-time circuit and rendezvous setup of about 7 seconds; that is excluded here because it happens once per session, not per request.

Concurrency 10 on a 2-core box was comfortable: median latency stayed under 1.7s and nothing dropped, so this is steady-state throughput, not the saturation ceiling. The 1% slow tail is inherent Tor circuit variance and would shrink with circuit pinning or pre-warming. For a search egress, sub-2s median with zero loss and zero blocks over a thousand real Google requests is a strong result.
